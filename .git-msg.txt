feat: one-to-one invitations, and a truthful "online"

An invitation to join a group can now be pushed to a person instead of handed to them as a link to
paste. It is a doorbell, not a chat: three message kinds exist (group-invite, join-notice, welcome),
and a fourth would mean the scope changed, so an unknown kind is refused loudly rather than stored
quietly.

DELIVERY IS CONFIRMED, NOT ASSUMED

`sent` is not `delivered`. The send tool reports only that the message is stored and whether the
recipient currently holds a connection; `deliveredAt` is written when the RECEIVER calls ack. The
failure this prevents is the one that kills trust in a messenger: reporting success for a message
nobody received.

Acks are idempotent (a retried ack must not move a timestamp the sender displays) and scoped to the
recipient (an ack by the wrong person would mark delivered a message its recipient never saw).

EVERY INVITATION MINTS A FRESH TOKEN

The store keeps only `tokenHash`, so an issued token cannot be read back. That looked like an
obstacle and is the right design: one person, one link, one `maxUses`, one thing to revoke.
Re-inviting mints another, which is truthful — the first may have leaked.

"ONLINE" NOW MEANS HOLDING A CONNECTION

`lastSeenAt` was only written when a member authenticated, posted or read. A colleague who started
their DSH, joined a group and then watched — the ordinary way to use a chat app — showed as offline
after two minutes, because watching writes nothing.

The subscribe skill now refreshes presence on a timer while its stream is open. A live socket
reported as offline is worse than no indicator at all, because it makes the reader stop believing
the indicator permanently. `store.touch` exists for exactly this: refresh the timestamp and change
nothing else, so a presence tick cannot put noise in the feed or move a sequence number.

Undelivered messages survive a restart; the message cap drops delivered ones before anything still
waiting to be read.

Verified: 420 tests (12 new: no-premature-delivery, self-send, unknown-kind, isolation, idempotent
ack, wrong-person ack, payload round-trip, presence window, and two restart cases), and end to end
against a live host — send, inbox with a working invite link, and ack all succeed.
