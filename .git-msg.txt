feat: a removed member finds out, because their local record goes

`group/remove` disturbed nothing on the removed person's machine. Their memberships.json
kept the record, so their panel kept listing the group, their unread count kept growing from
nothing, and every action failed with `not_member` — a code they never saw. From their side
the feature looked broken rather than their access having ended.

The runtime now notices the two codes that mean "your membership is gone" and deletes the
local record:

    not_member      the credential is no longer valid for the group
    unknown_group   the group is gone, or the cache points at a host that lacks it

Centralised in `call()` rather than per handler: the alternative is the same eight lines in
twenty places, and one of them gets forgotten. The error keeps its code and gains
`membershipDropped`, so a caller can tell "you were removed" from "that call failed".

Two deliberate narrowings:

- it removes exactly one (groupId, sessionId) pair. A failure on one group says nothing
  about the others, and a broader sweep would turn one stale credential into a wiped ledger.
- it reaps only on those two codes. A network blip or a rate limit says nothing about
  membership, and dropping a valid membership over one bad afternoon is worse than the
  confusion being fixed.

I considered keeping a "recent departures" ring so the panel could say "you were removed
from X", and dropped it: the panel renders from the injected snapshot, so a cleaned record
already removes the group, which is what happened. The extra field would be one more thing
to keep correct for one line of text.

Verification: 345 tests (8 new), and end to end with a real ledger — only the failing group
is dropped, the other membership is untouched.
