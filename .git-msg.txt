feat: remove, rename, archive — the three things running a group needs

A group could be created and joined but never maintained. The only remedy for a wrong name
was to create a new group and lose its history, which nobody would do, so groups kept wrong
names; and the only way to deal with a person who should no longer be there was to abandon
the group.

The three share one shape — owner credential, one mutation, one system entry — so they got
one security review rather than three passes at the same question.

`remove` keeps the target's messages. Deleting them would rewrite the conversation: a
reader would see people answering somebody who never spoke. A system line records the
removal because the remaining members will notice the absence, and silence in a group about
people is how rumours start. The owner cannot remove themselves — that is `leave`, which
transfers ownership, and mixing the two would leave a group owned by someone not in it.

`rename` covers name and topic. Empty topic clears it; a no-op rename reports "nothing
changed" and writes no feed entry, because a maintenance action must not add noise.

`archive` is readable-but-not-writable and absent from the directory. Deliberately not a
delete: a finished project's conversation is worth keeping, and reopening a misclick must
be possible. Reads stay allowed because the first reason to archive is "we are done" and
the second is "what did we decide".

Verification: 337 tests (16 new), and end to end on a real host over A2A — removed member
gets not_member, archived group gets group_archived, history survives both.
